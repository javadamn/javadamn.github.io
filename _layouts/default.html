I like asking biological questions that do not fit neatly into one box.

Javad Aminian

I’m a PhD candidate in Computational Biology and Biomathematics at UC Berkeley, where I work at the intersection of computational biology, systems science, and machine learning. I build models and learning systems to understand how biological systems organize, adapt, and respond, and, importantly, how we can turn that understanding into better decisions.

My work brings together different computational tools, optimization, and AI to tackle biological problems from multiple angles. I’m especially interested in what happens when we stop treating biology as a collection of isolated pieces and instead try to understand the rules connecting them.

I also enjoy borrowing ideas from places that, at first glance, seem far away from biology. Decision theory and microeconomics, for example, offer surprisingly useful ways to think about biological trade-offs, uncertainty, and choice. Teaching Microeconomic Analysis for Business Decisions at Berkeley’s Haas School of Business gave me another perspective on how these ideas can sharpen the questions we ask in biology.

In short: I like building things, connecting ideas, and asking “what if?”, especially when the answer leads somewhere unexpected.
